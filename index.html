You get a grade, a breakdown of where the points went, and the single change worth making next time. Not a wall of statistics: one instruction, on the screen you open before you train.

The loop

Three things happen
every time you train

There's no dashboard to configure and no weekly review to sit down for. The loop runs on the same screen you open before your first set.

1 STEP 01

Open Today

Your split is already on a rotation — six-day push / pull / legs A and B out of the box, or whatever you build in the Program tab. The app knows which day you're on, and what you lifted last time you were here.

2 STEP 02

Log the sets

Weight, reps, and one tap for how hard it felt. The rest timer starts itself when you tick a set. Warm-ups, supersets and alternating movements are all handled in the same rows — nothing to set up mid-workout.

3 STEP 03

Read what comes back

A grade the moment you finish, with the one change worth making. Your muscle map updates as the sets land, your estimated 1RMs move, and any lift that has gone four weeks without progress gets called out.

What it believes

Three rules,
held everywhere

Nothing invented quietly

Where the app doesn't know something, it asks or it says so. It won't fill a gap with an average and let you assume it was measured.

Landmarks, not laws

The volume targets are population averages with wide individual variation, and they're labelled that way everywhere they appear. Yours may sit higher or lower.

Your log is yours

Nothing you've already logged is ever rewritten. Edit the programme freely — the history stays exactly as you recorded it, and the change is written down.

Questions

Before you start

Do I have to follow a specific programme?

No. It ships with a six-day push / pull / legs rotation that's already been measured against the volume landmarks muscle by muscle, but every exercise, set count and rep range is editable — and you can build your own split from scratch in the Program tab.

Do I have to rate every set?

No. Rating effort costs one tap per set and it's what drives the grade, the load suggestions and stall detection — but you can turn it off, and the set rows lose the column entirely. The session still grades, with a little less to go on.

Where do the volume landmarks come from?

The MEV / MAV / MRV framework popularised by Mike Israetel and Renaissance Periodization, expressed in hard sets per muscle per week — the unit the research on training volume is written in. They're population averages with wide individual variation, so the app treats them as a starting frame rather than a rule, and says as much wherever it shows them.

What counts as a hard set?

A working set taken close enough to failure to drive adaptation. Warm-ups don't count toward your weekly volume, don't reach the muscle map, and don't affect the grade — they're logged, but they're kept out of the totals so the numbers mean what they say.

Does it work offline?

Yes. Gym basements are the normal case, not the edge case. Everything you log is written locally first, and syncing is something the app does when it can rather than something it needs to function.

How much is it?

$15, paid once, for one person — not $15 a month. There's no renewal, no tier above it and no feature held back, and updates come with it. That's the entire commercial arrangement.

Do you want my email address?

No — and there's no password and no sign-in with anyone else's account either. You give it a name and it hands you an ID and a recovery key to save. If you'd rather nothing left the device at all, your account and everything in it can stay local.
