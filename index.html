The product

Everything you need to
train on purpose

Most logs are a notebook with a database behind it. This one reads the numbers back to you: what moved, what stalled, and which muscle is quietly getting nothing.

Bring your plan in as it is

Hand it a coach's PDF, a spreadsheet, a screenshot or a photo of the sheet in your gym bag. It reads the document, pulls out the workouts, exercises, sets, rep ranges and any instructions written into it — deloads, supersets, dropsets — and asks you about anything ambiguous rather than guessing. Nothing lands in your workouts until you've seen it laid out and said yes.

hypertrophy-block-3.pdf ✓

Sessions that grade themselves

Every workout is scored against the last time you did the same lifts — load, reps, effort and whether you actually rested. You get a grade and, more usefully, the single change worth making next time.

A−Load +2Rest −0.8

Volume you can actually see

Hard sets per muscle per week — the unit the research is written in — laid against minimum effective, maximum adaptive and maximum recoverable volume. Sit under the line two weeks running and the muscle gets flagged.

Progression it can defend

Load suggestions come from your own history, not a formula applied blind. It spots stalls, calibrates a starting weight on any lift with no baseline, and keeps a change log of every edit you make to the programme — so when a rep range moves from 6–10 to 12–15 and your estimated 1RM drops by design, the grade knows that isn't you going backwards.

No jargon left standing

Every technical term in the app is underlined. Tap it and you get a plain-English entry — what it means, and why it changes what you do.

Keep this lift near RPE 8, or roughly two reps in reserve.

Reports worth printing

Your whole programme, or your whole history, laid out as a document you can hand to a coach or a physio — including what each muscle receives per cycle.

An account that asks for nothing

No email, no password, no sign-in with anything. You give it a name, and it gives you an ID and a recovery key to keep. That's the whole setup — and if you'd rather it never left the device at all, it doesn't have to.

How it works

Three steps to a
programme that runs itself

The setup is the part every other log gets wrong. Here it's a conversation, and you approve everything before it becomes real.

STEP 01

Hand over your plan

Upload the PDF, the spreadsheet or a photo of the page. It checks the document is readable before it does anything else, then pulls out the workouts, exercises, sets and rep ranges — along with any rules written into it.

STEP 02

Answer what it can't infer

A few short questions, only where the document is genuinely silent — how you want sides handled, whether to generate warm-ups, what to grade effort against if your plan sets no targets. It won't invent an answer quietly.

STEP 03

Check it, then train

You see the whole thing laid out day by day, with anything it couldn't match exactly called out so you can fix it. Say yes and your first session is waiting on the Today tab. Everything stays editable afterwards.

Questions

Before you start

Do I need a plan already?

It's the fastest way in — the importer exists so you never retype a coach's programme. But you can also build a split by hand in the Program tab, and everything the importer produces is editable there anyway.

What can the importer actually read?

PDFs, images (including photos and screenshots), plain text, Markdown and CSV — or you can paste the plan in as text. It also recognises an Element 26 export, which is how you restore a backup through the same button.

What if it reads something wrong?

You see the full plan before it's added, and anything the exercise database couldn't match exactly is called out with somewhere to correct it. Nothing reaches your workouts until you've approved it — and once your history exists, nothing the importer does can change what you already logged.

Where do the volume landmarks come from?

The MEV / MAV / MRV framework popularised by Mike Israetel and Renaissance Periodization, expressed in hard sets per muscle per week. They're population averages with wide individual variation — the app treats them as a starting frame rather than a rule, and says as much wherever it shows them.

Do I have to rate every set?

No. Rating effort costs one tap per set and it's what drives the grade, the load suggestions and stall detection — but you can turn it off, and the set rows lose the column entirely. The session still grades, with a little less to go on.

Do you want my email address?

No — and there's no password and no sign-in with anyone else's account either. You give it a name and it hands you an ID and a recovery key to save. If you'd rather nothing left the device at all, your account and everything in it can stay local.
